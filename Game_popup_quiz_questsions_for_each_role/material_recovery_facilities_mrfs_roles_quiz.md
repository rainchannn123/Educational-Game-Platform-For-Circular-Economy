# Material Recovery Facilities (MRFs) Roles: Multiple-Choice Questions

This quiz is designed for high school to undergraduate-level students to test their understanding of the mechanical processes, sorting technologies, operational challenges, and responsibilities of **Material Recovery Facilities (MRFs)** within a circular economy.

### Questions

#### 1. What is the primary function of an MRF?

A) Garbage collection
B) Sorting, cleaning, and baling recyclables
C) Manufacturing consumer goods
D) Setting waste laws

#### 2. What is the difference between a "clean MRF" and a "dirty MRF"?

A) Hazardous vs. organic waste handling
B) Source-separated vs. mixed municipal waste processing
C) Robotic vs. manual labor usage
D) Indoor vs. outdoor facilities

#### 3. Which device extracts ferrous metals (like steel cans)?

A) Eddy current separator
B) Optical sorter
C) Overband magnet
D) Air classifier

#### 4. How are non-ferrous metals (like aluminum) separated?

A) Eddy current separator
B) UV scanner
C) Manual sorting
D) Chemical bath

#### 5. What is the main purpose of optical sorters?

A) Compressing bales
B) Identifying plastics and paper via infrared light
C) Removing glass shards
D) Weighing trucks

#### 6. What does a trommel screen do?

A) Melts plastics
B) Separates items by size
C) Shreds cardboard
D) Compacts waste

#### 7. What is "residue"?

A) High-grade aluminum
B) Non-recyclable trash sent to landfills
C) Plastic washwater
D) Baled cardboard

#### 8. Why is contamination control critical at an MRF?

A) To meet buyer purity standards
B) To speed up trucks
C) To lower insurance
D) To remove magnets

#### 9. What is the main purpose of baling?

A) Hiding defects
B) Compressing for transport and sale
C) Preparing for landfill
D) Cooling machinery

#### 10. What is a ballistic separator used for?

A) Separating 2D items from 3D items
B) Crushing glass
C) Detecting radiation
D) Sorting e-waste

#### 11. Why are glass containers handled carefully?

A) Too lightweight to sort
B) Abrasive damage and paper contamination
C) Zero economic value
D) Melts optical lenses

#### 12. What does OCC stand for?

A) Organic Compost Component
B) Old Corrugated Cardboard
C) Optimized Container
D) Out-of-Cycle Collection

#### 13. What problem do plastic film bags cause?

A) Increase aluminum recovery
B) Wrap around shafts and cause downtime
C) Improve paper quality
D) Lubricate belts

#### 14. What is the role of manual sorters?

A) Designing bins
B) Removing large contaminants and dangerous items
C) Negotiating broker prices
D) Driving trucks

#### 15. What is an air classifier used for?

A) Separating lightweight items with air streams
B) Cooling motors
C) Testing room air
D) Removing glass dust

#### 16. What is a bale specification?

A) Legal dumping permit
B) Industry standards for weight, density, and purity limits
C) MRF blueprint
D) Zoning rule

#### 17. How do MRFs track operational efficiency?

A) Recovery rates, throughput, and loss percentages
B) Daily employee count
C) City population growth
D) Weather patterns

#### 18. Why do commodity prices experience volatility?

A) Daily law changes
B) Global supply, demand, and virgin material price shifts
C) Seasonal breakdowns
D) Paper decay

#### 19. What is a secondary sort line used for?

A) Polishing sorted streams for max purity
B) Dumping waste
C) Washing trucks
D) Staff training

#### 20. What is a major limitation of optical sorting?

A) Needs dark rooms
B) Struggles with dark, black, or soiled plastics
C) Only works on organics
D) Requires water immersion

#### 21. How are hazardous items (batteries/propane) handled?

A) Melted with plastics
B) Workers manually extract them during pre-sort
C) Shipped to paper mills
D) Ignored

#### 22. What is "throughput"?

A) Profit per ton
B) Total volume of material processed over time
C) Landfill percentage
D) Truck highway speed

#### 23. What is the difference between PET and HDPE?

A) Glass vs. metal
B) Different polymer resin types needing separate sorting
C) Organic vs. inorganic
D) Hazardous vs. inert

#### 24. Why is moisture control important for paper?

A) Wet paper ruins fiber quality and lowers market value
B) Causes electrical fires
C) Speeds up optical sorters
D) Increases bale weight

#### 25. How do eddy current rotors work?

A) Gravity drops
B) Rotating magnetic fields inducing currents to repel non-ferrous metals
C) Freezing aluminum
D) Suction fans

#### 26. What is the primary financial risk for private MRFs?

A) Water shortage
B) Dropping commodity prices paired with high municipal contamination
C) Overstaffing
D) Landscaping rules

#### 27. How does an MRF bridge municipalities and brokers?

A) Shipping retail goods
B) Processing messy trash into clean, baled commodities for global trade
C) Setting national taxes
D) Auditing household cans

#### 28. What is "wish-cycling"?

A) Recycling too much aluminum
B) Tying trash to bikes
C) Tossing unrecyclable items in bins hoping they work, causing sorting hazards
D) Cleaning bottles too well

#### 29. What is the goal of densifying materials?

A) Making bales heavier for dumps
B) Reducing shipping volume and freight costs
C) Changing chemical makeup
D) Dissolving into liquids

#### 30. What role do AI and robotics play in modern MRFs?

A) Replacing administrative staff
B) Powering robotic arms for fast, precise sorting
C) Driving autonomous trucks
D) Replacing electricity
