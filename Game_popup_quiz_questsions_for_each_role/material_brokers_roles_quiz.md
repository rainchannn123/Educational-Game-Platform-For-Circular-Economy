# Material Brokers Roles: Multiple-Choice Questions

This quiz is designed for high school to undergraduate-level students to test their understanding of market liquidity, price discovery, logistics, risk management, and the responsibilities of **brokers** (material traders and intermediaries) within a circular economy.

### Questions

#### 1. What is the primary role of a broker in the recycling supply chain?

A) Collecting garbage
B) Sorting mixed waste
C) Connecting suppliers with global buyers
D) Enforcing zoning laws

#### 2. How do brokers help MRFs manage market risks?

A) Buying heavy machinery
B) Securing contracts and hedging volatility
C) Paying municipal taxes
D) Taking over collection routes

#### 3. What does "price discovery" mean in secondary commodity trading?

A) Finding hidden items in bins
B) Determining fair market value from supply and demand
C) Setting collection fees
D) Valuing real estate

#### 4. Why are international waste trade regulations (like the Basel Convention) critical for brokers?

A) Setting cargo ship speeds
B) Ensuring legal, sound cross-border trade
C) Dictating landfill taxes
D) Banning recycling

#### 5. What happens when a manufacturing buyer rejects a shipment due to high contamination?

A) Arbitrating disputes and negotiating re-grading
B) Burning the material immediately
C) Jailing the municipality
D) Forcing the shipping company to buy a factory

#### 6. What is a "spot market" in secondary commodities?

A) A retail used-goods store
B) Trading immediately at current daily prices
C) A hazardous waste drop-off zone
D) A temporary storage yard

#### 7. How do brokers provide market intelligence to processing facilities?

A) Testing municipal water
B) Sharing trends on high-demand materials
C) Auditing household habits
D) Designing sorting robots

#### 8. Why do brokers need deep knowledge of global freight logistics?

A) Driving trucks personally
B) Managing multi-mode freight cost-effectively
C) Bypassing weight stations
D) Avoiding insurance

#### 9. What is the main driver of commodity price volatility for recycled plastics?

A) Daily weather patterns
B) Virgin petroleum price and demand fluctuations
C) City council elections
D) Paper mill capacity shifts

#### 10. What is a "letter of credit" in broker-led international trade?

A) A school passing certificate
B) A bank guarantee ensuring payment upon shipping proof
C) A dumping permit
D) A bulk cardboard discount coupon

#### 11. How do brokers handle currency exchange risks when trading globally?

A) Refusing foreign currency
B) Using financial hedging and multi-currency contracts
C) Paying only in gold
D) Relying on fixed local laws

#### 12. Why might a broker advise an MRF to upgrade its sorting technology?

A) Increasing noise levels
B) Meeting strict mill purity specifications
C) Consuming more electricity
D) Slowing production

#### 13. What is the key difference between a broker and an MRF?

A) Commerce and logistics vs. physical sorting
B) City workers vs. individual workers
C) Hazardous vs. paper handling
D) No difference

#### 14. What is contamination arbitration?

A) Settling property disputes
B) Resolving purity and grading disagreements
C) Testing landfill water
D) Fining residents

#### 15. Why is demand for Old Corrugated Cardboard (OCC) heavily tied to e-commerce?

A) Higher shipping box volume for mills
B) Cardboard powering delivery vans
C) Retailers burning cardboard for heat
D) Online shoppers recycling plastics

#### 16. How do brokers mitigate the risk of a buyer defaulting on payment?

A) Trusting customers blindly
B) Conducting credit checks and secure financial terms
C) Withholding tracking numbers
D) Demanding cash in person

#### 17. What role do brokers play as "liquidity catalysts"?

A) Melting plastics into liquids
B) Ensuring steady material flow between supply and demand
C) Managing water plants
D) Pumping landfill oil

#### 18. What is a broker's responsibility regarding customs clearance?

A) Inspecting truck engines
B) Managing documentation, tariffs, and compliance
C) Collecting park taxes
D) Weighing drivers

#### 19. Why do some countries ban certain imported scrap plastics?

A) Protecting ecosystems from unrecyclable waste
B) Encouraging citizens to buy plastic
C) Increasing broker shipping costs
D) Eliminating manufacturing

#### 20. How do brokers leverage intermodal transport (rail and ocean freight)?

A) Shipping small boxes locally
B) Moving bulk volumes efficiently long-distance
C) Bypassing environmental laws
D) Storing materials indefinitely

#### 21. What is a material broker's primary source of revenue?

A) Property taxes
B) Commissions, margins, or logistics fees
C) Tipping fees
D) Government recycling subsidies

#### 22. How do global economic downturns typically affect secondary commodity brokers?

A) Mill demand drops, causing price crashes
B) Demand increases exponentially
C) Operations remain unaffected
D) Shipping costs drop to zero

#### 23. What is "feedstock" in the context of material brokerage?

A) Animal food from scraps
B) Raw secondary materials supplied to mills
C) Truck fuel
D) Grid electricity

#### 24. Why must brokers verify bale weights and moisture levels?

A) Preventing disputes and meeting transport limits
B) Checking if plastic is waterproof
C) Calculating population size
D) Determining weather

#### 25. What is a long-term supply contract in material brokerage?

A) A verbal neighbor promise
B) Fixed-volume structured agreements over time
C) A daily spot trade
D) A warehouse lease

#### 26. How do brokers support closed-loop manufacturing goals?

A) Finding reliable recycled inputs for supply chains
B) Encouraging single-use virgin plastics
C) Destroying used products
D) Blocking trade

#### 27. What is "triangular trade" in secondary commodities?

A) Shipping in triangle boxes
B) Direct shipping managed from a third hub
C) Recycling three metals at once
D) Trading between three cities

#### 28. Why is transparent communication vital between MRFs and brokers?

A) Hiding trade secrets
B) Aligning expectations on volume and quality
C) Eliminating contracts
D) Setting property taxes

#### 29. How do regional trade agreements affect recycling brokerages?

A) Simplifying logistics and reducing tariffs
B) Banning material movement
C) Replacing brokers with government workers
D) Eliminating shipping

#### 30. What is the ultimate economic function of brokers in a circular system?

A) Maximizing resource value via matching supply and demand
B) Disposing waste in oceans
C) Eliminating recycling facilities
D) Controlling water supplies
